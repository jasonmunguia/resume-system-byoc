# Sub-skill 3 — Bullet Creator

**Input:** the allocation plan from `evidence-mapper.md` — one experience at a
time, each bullet already assigned an evidentiary job.
**Output:** finished bullets that survive the tests below.

Work **one experience at a time** unless told otherwise. Never regenerate a whole
resume in one pass; that removes the human from a set of judgment calls only they
can make.

## Primary signal

Before drafting, name:

- **PRIMARY SIGNAL** — the single thing the recruiter should remember.
- **SECONDARY SIGNALS** — what else can ride along *without diluting the primary*.

This determines word order. The first words are premium real estate; put the
strongest role-relevant signal there.

> 📌 **On "one qualification per bullet."** The rule is one *purpose*, not one
> detectable concept. A bullet may evidence several qualifications as long as one
> clearly dominates. The failure mode is a bullet with **no dominant signal** —
> three things half-proven. Do not chase a literal count.

## Content components

Pick a subset; no bullet needs all of them.

**Ownership** (what I was responsible for) · **Action** (what I personally did) ·
**Insight** (what I figured out) · **Mechanism** (how the result was produced) ·
**Scale** (how large/difficult/consequential) · **Stakeholder** (who I influenced
or presented to) · **Artifact** (what I built, analyzed, negotiated) ·
**Decision** (what organizational decision changed) · **Outcome** (what
objectively changed) · **Purpose/Function** (what the thing was designed to do)

## There is no universal formula

Do not enforce *always lead with the metric*, *always start with the verb*, or
any single template. Architecture follows evidence. Uniform reasoning is the
goal; uniform syntax is not — and variation actually strengthens a page, because
different bullets surface different kinds of evidence.

> ⚠️ This supersedes HHH p.24–26's fixed *What → How → Result/Reason*. That
> formula is a good default for high-volume corporate applications; it is
> Architecture 1/5 below, not the only shape. For competitive campus and
> consulting programs, conditional architecture wins.

### Architecture 1 — Result first
`RESULT → HOW/WHY → SUPPORTING SCALE`
Use when the metric is exceptional, maps directly to the qualification, reads
instantly, and the causality is defensible. **Do not result-front merely because
a number exists.**

### Architecture 2 — Ownership / trust first
`OWNED X → SCOPE OR STAKEHOLDER → ACTION → OUTCOME`
Use when what you were entrusted with is itself the evidence: unusually senior
responsibility, stakeholder caliber, decision authority, client ownership. For a
stakeholder-management qualification this can beat leading with the metric.

### Architecture 3 — Build / artifact first
`BUILT X → WHAT IT DID → HOW/WHERE USED → OUTCOME`
For technical fluency, analytics, automation, product, data. Never stop at
*"built an AI system that does X"* — establish who used it, at what scale, what
process or decision changed, what followed.

### Architecture 4 — Insight / diagnosis first
`IDENTIFIED X → EVIDENCE USED → ACTION INFLUENCED → OUTCOME`
For strategy, problem solving, product sense, business judgment, ambiguity. This
architecture says *I determined what should be done*, not *I executed
instructions*.

### Architecture 5 — Execution / deal first
`NEGOTIATED / CLOSED / SOURCED / LAUNCHED → WITH WHOM → HOW → OUTCOME`
For GTM, sales, business development, partnerships, deployment, commercialization.

**Selection rule:** whichever is strongest *and* most relevant leads.

## Mechanism usually earns its space

Do not reflexively collapse `action → how → outcome` into `action → metric`.

> "Raised $550K." — proves outcome.
> "Raised $550K after identifying a stronger use case, validating demand through
> 150+ prospective-customer interviews, and influencing a product pivot." —
> additionally proves research, analysis, customer discovery, judgment, influence.

Ask: **does explaining how this happened add evidence for a supplied
qualification?** If yes, keep it. If not, compress it.

## Function vs. intent vs. outcome

- **Function** — what the thing actually did. Often essential to comprehension:
  *"...to rank 650+ seller homes by equity, motivation, and profit margin"* is
  what makes the model evaluable.
- **Intent** — what you hoped it would achieve. Weak on its own: *"Built AI
  system to increase sales"* states a goal.
- **Outcome** — what actually happened. Strongest: *"...increasing sales 15%."*

Include purpose when it makes the mechanism understandable, proves a
qualification, or explains why an obscure action mattered. Cut it when the
outcome makes it obvious, when it is only aspiration, or when it repeats.

## Activity is not automatically evidence

Calls, meetings, emails, decks, texts, workshops, weekly syncs are usually
*channels*. `"gathered feedback via calls, texts, and in-person workshops"`
compresses to `"synthesized client feedback"` — unless the channel itself proves
the qualification (*"led workshops for entire customer teams"* matters for
customer education, implementation, change management).

Related: **"created a PowerPoint" is an artifact; "presented recommendations that
changed a decision" is organizational influence.** Prefer the latter unless
producing the artifact is the job.

## Technical translation

A recurring, distinct competency: moving between customer/business need and
technical/product implementation — customer feedback → engineering priorities,
factory problem → robotic solution, product usage → feature development. Name it
when the supplied qualifications reward it.

## The four tests

**Stranger Test.** The bullet must land for an intelligent reader who has never
met you, never heard of the company, doesn't know the product or internal
vocabulary, and is scanning. They should get: what you did · what you owned ·
the problem · scale and with whom · how · what decision changed · the result ·
why it matters. Not all eight every time — but **no critical causal link may
depend on insider knowledge.**

*Do not over-correct.* Stranger clarity is not restating what the resume already
establishes. If the header reads **Northwind — AI Video Analytics**, the
bullet says *"trained their teams"*, not *"trained their teams on Northwind"*. The
stranger needs sufficient information, not redundant information.

**Causality Test.** Every metric must be defensible. If attribution is direct:
*increased, reduced, generated, closed, cut, tripled*. If shared or indirect:
*contributed to, helped drive, supported, informed*. Never inflate a verb —
truth that survives an interview beats a verb that collapses under one question.

**Personal-Ownership Test.** Separate company outcome, team outcome, and your
contribution. Don't imply sole ownership of a team result; equally, don't weaken
genuine ownership with unnecessary *"helped"*. Use the strongest accurate claim.

**Interview-Defensibility Test.** Every finished bullet must survive: *What
exactly did you do? How? What made it difficult? How was the metric calculated?
How much is attributable to you? Who decided? Why did your intervention work?
What alternatives were considered? How did you know?*

## Metrics

Choose the metric that best proves the *supplied qualification* — do not
reflexively prefer revenue.

**Commercial** revenue · ARR · pipeline · contract value · retention · renewal ·
conversion · close rate — **Operations** time saved · cost · margin · throughput ·
cycle time · resource efficiency — **Product** retention · adoption · activation ·
usage · customer success — **Technical** latency · accuracy · deployment scale ·
users · automation time · reliability — **People/scale** team size · interviews ·
clients · people trained · industries · locations

## Prestige and names

Recognizable organizations communicate trust, client sophistication, difficulty,
commercial scale, stakeholder caliber. Use them where they strengthen that
inference — but prestige is **supporting evidence, not decoration**. Three names
usually carry the same signal as six. For a specialized target role, a
less-famous but more role-relevant company can be the better name.

## Mirror concepts, not labels

Conceptual mirroring beats word mirroring. If the qualification reads *"uses
quantitative and qualitative research to identify unmet needs"* and you ran 150+
interviews that produced a pivot, **the accomplishment already mirrors it** —
adding *"demonstrated user empathy and product sense"* weakens it. Show, don't
label.

> 📌 **This is word-level inception** — see "The Inception Method" in
> `SKILL.md` for the full two-level picture. Mirror their **action
> vocabulary**, never their **trait vocabulary**. *"Scoped engagements in new
> industries"* borrows their verb-noun for something you actually did.
> *"Demonstrated stakeholder management"* borrows their label for a trait —
> that is keyword stuffing. Their noun, your number; never their adjective.
> The other half — matching a role's *functional sequence*, not just its
> vocabulary — is sequence-level inception, in `qualification-finder.md`'s
> job-cycle section.

**Translate terminology only when it describes the same thing.** *"Product
refinement memo"* may genuinely be product requirements, customer requirements,
a decision memo, or a spec — determine what the document actually did first.
Never rename it *"product spec"* just because a PM posting says *spec*.

## Ask before writing around a gap

If an accomplishment looks highly relevant but incomplete, **interrogate it
rather than writing around it**. Ask only questions that materially improve
relevance, evidence, specificity, credibility, scale, mechanism, or impact:

*What changed afterward? How many people used it? Who was the decision maker?
What was your personal role? Was it implemented? How was the metric measured?
How many clients or accounts? How large was the team? What happened before vs.
after? Why did the company care? What did you personally build? What decision
did your analysis change?*

## Punctuation

No semicolons, no em dashes. Both read as inconsistent across fonts and ATS
parsers, and both are easy to avoid: a comma plus an infinitive clause
("...to cut R&D time and accelerate deployments") does the same connective job
a dash would, without the character.

A relative clause ("that...") often works too, but check what it actually
attaches to. "And more that cut R&D time" reads as if "more" is the thing that
cut R&D time, not the deal list. If a comma-plus-infinitive removes that
ambiguity, prefer it.

## Compression order

Delete in roughly this order — never randomly:

1. low-value procedural channels
2. context the section or company header already supplies
3. filler adjectives and adverbs
4. repeated explanations
5. unnecessary connectors (*"then"*)
6. excess company examples
7. secondary mechanism
8. secondary qualification signal

Preserve: primary qualification evidence · primary signal · causal mechanism
where relevant · important scale · decision maker where relevant · outcome ·
stranger comprehension · **grammar**. Never produce malformed English to save a
line.

## Showing drafts

Do not generate synonym variants. Offer alternatives **only when there is a real
strategic tradeoff** — e.g. *Version A leads with revenue because the commercial
result is strongest; Version B leads with Fortune 500 relationship ownership
because the stakeholder qualification is strongest.* Explain the tradeoff, then
recommend one against the supplied qualifications.

## Internal review — run silently unless asked

Target qualification · primary signal · architecture (why is this fact first?) ·
burger test · stranger test · personal ownership · mechanism · purpose · scale ·
decision-maker · outcome · causality · redundancy · compression · interview test.

## Voice

The strongest developed bullets read like **miniature causal case studies**:
what I did + how/why it worked + who/what scale + what decision changed +
measurable result. Not a rigid formula — the characteristic is **high
information density, visible causality, personal agency.**

Default persona: high-agency operator · analytical problem solver · technically
fluent business builder · communicator who turns insight into action.

**Role relevance outranks the persona.** If the supplied role rewards a different
dimension, tailor to the role.
